Although, Though, Even Though & Despite

Four words, one job: showing contrast. There's really just one question to ask yourself each time — this page shows you what it is.

group 1 — a full sentence comes after group 2 — just a word comes after
Part 1

The One Question to Ask

Don't think about meaning — all four words mean the same thing ("even so..."). Think about grammar: what comes right after the word?

The trick / O truque
Is there a verb conjugated right after? (is, was, rains, studied...)
→ use Although / Even Though / Though
Is there only a word or an -ing right after? (the rain, studying...)
→ use Despite / In Spite Of

Se depois da palavra vem uma frase completa (com verbo conjugado) → although / even though / though. Se depois vem só uma palavra ou um verbo em -ing → despite / in spite of.

Although group 1
Comes before a full sentence piece: subject + verb
  • The most neutral/normal option. Good for writing and speaking.
  • Goes at the start or in the middle of the sentence.
Although it was raining, we went for a walk.

Embora estivesse chovendo, nós saímos para caminhar. — a palavra que você já conhece em português ("embora") é exatamente isso.

Even Though group 1
Comes before a full sentence piece: subject + verb
  • Same grammar as although — just a little stronger, for when the contrast is surprising.
  • Goes at the start or in the middle of the sentence.
Even though he studied all night, he still failed the exam.

Mesmo que / mesmo assim — use quando o contraste é mais forte ou mais surpreendente.

Though group 1
Comes before a full sentence piece: subject + verb
  • Means exactly the same as although, but sounds more casual — great for conversation.
  • Special extra use: it can go alone, at the end of a sentence, like a comment you add after.
We went for a walk. It was raining, though.

"Though" no final da frase é muito comum na fala — funciona como um "mas, viu?" jogado no fim.

Despite / In Spite Of group 2
Comes before just a word or an -ing word — never before a conjugated verb
  • Same meaning as the group above — the difference is only grammar, not meaning.
  • "In spite of" and "despite" mean exactly the same thing.
Despite the rain, we went for a walk.
Despite studying all night, he failed the exam.

Repare: depois de "despite" nunca vem um verbo conjugado (was, is, studied) — só um substantivo ou um verbo com -ing.

Watch out: "despite of" doesn't exist in English. Say only despite — or, if you want "of", say in spite of.

Erro comum: nunca use "despite of". É "despite" (sem "of") ou "in spite of".
